## Residual target and its limits

For each real transition (s,b,r,s',d), r = -intervalTTT/100, gamma = 1, and d denotes true termination only. Let b' = P(a' + D*pi_target(s')). Set Phi(absorbing terminal) = 0 explicitly, keep Phi fixed, and stop gradients through the complete target:

```text
Q_i(s,b) = Phi(s) + A_i(s,b)
y_A = r - Phi(s)                         if d
y_A = r + Phi(s') + min_j A_target,j(s',b') - Phi(s)   otherwise
L_A = sum_i mean[(A_i(s,b) - stopgrad(y_A))^2]
y_Q = r                                 if d
y_Q = r + min_j Q_target,j(s',b')         otherwise
```

Thus Phi(s) + y_A is exactly the usual Q target. With terminal Phi zero, the transformed rewards telescope to G_t - Phi(s_t). This is a change of numerical representation, not new optimality evidence. A is a residual, not necessarily an advantage with A(s,pi0(s)) = 0. At the final ACTION state Q = r, not zero. Mask terminal continuation before network evaluation; an all-zero terminal observation does not itself force a neural Phi to zero.

Fit Phi only to observed local-behavior returns G_t = sum_{k=t}^{74} r_k as an initializer. These exclude warmup TTT and include all remaining real intervals. Phi need not equal V^pi0 or V^pi1; TD must learn its correction. Freeze its weights, normalization, and hash throughout the comparison and initial continuation. Never relabel local returns as carry, new-policy, nearby-action, or optimal Q targets.

At fixed s, dQ/db = dA/db: subtracting a state-only baseline cannot certify an action gradient. An inaccurate Phi can leave a large residual and can worsen conditioning. Its utility here is a falsifiable numerical hypothesis, not an asserted variance reduction guarantee. No external-theory claim is needed for these algebraic identities.

## Falsifiable calibration and admission gates

These are proposed engineering thresholds to freeze BEFORE execution, not established statistical confidence bounds. Apply them separately to both seeds and both reconstructed Q heads (also report min-Q). All errors below are in scaled reward units; do not score A alone against G.

- Boundary gate: maximum |Q_i(s,b_observed)-r| <= 0.5 across all ten true-terminal rows, reporting five fitted and five held-out terminals separately. Terminals identify only their recorded state/request pairs.
- Carry gate: on each withheld carry state compare Q_i(s,P(a)) with its actual remaining carry return G_t. Define e_t = |Q_i-G_t| / max(1,|G_t|). Require mean e <= 0.05 in EVERY scenario and each time band 0-24, 25-49, 50-74; require max e <= 0.10 across all carry rows. Report signed bias and raw MAE/max alongside this normalization.
- Selection rule: if plain Q passes both gates in both seeds, keep plain Q for simplicity. Otherwise admit Phi only if it passes both gates in both seeds, reduces equally weighted mean carry e by at least 25% versus plain in EACH seed/head, and worsens no scenario/time-band mean e. If neither qualifies, admit neither and diagnose the recorded failure; do not lengthen the run automatically. If an arm is nonfinite, reject it and do not manufacture comparative percentages.
- Passing admits only the selected numerical learner to a bounded provisional training-policy probe. It does not admit unrestricted offline actor optimization, a production policy, or canonical scoring. Use predeclared seed 7101 for that next candidate, not the better-looking seed.

The carry gate is independent of optimizer targets and has the correct pi0 continuation. It is still only a sampled return check on five held-out trajectories: carry/local share demand profiles and some prefix states, and overlapping returns are correlated. It is not five independent new demand tests or 375 independent samples. Local-behavior MC fit, small own-target TD residual, twin agreement, and projection consistency cannot independently validate another continuation or action ordering.

## Decision refinement: initialize returns, then obtain on-policy evidence

This refinement supersedes the proposed paired fresh-Q comparison, exclusion of carry from fitting, and mandatory all-band 5% calibration gates above. It remains conditional on completion/authentication of all collection and coverage screens. The parent will freeze the exact specification; no learner or run is admitted by this report.

The target schedule deserves a substantial qualification. With tau = 0.005 and one target update per two critic updates, 750 critic updates give 375 target updates. For a fixed online parameter vector, the initial target coefficient is (1-0.005)^375, approximately 0.153: the target has incorporated approximately 84.7% of that fixed parameter change. Its half-life is log(0.5)/log(0.995) = approximately 138.3 target updates, or 276.6 critic updates; its e-folding time is approximately 199.5 target updates, or 399 critic updates. Reaching 95% of a fixed change takes approximately 1196 critic updates. These are parameter EMA calculations from `work/sdmpc_rl_multi_20260929/td3.py:63` and `:198`, not measured Q-error decay.

Thus 750 updates span only approximately 1.88 EMA time constants. A first-interval return contains 74 subsequent rewards as well as its immediate reward; fresh one-step TD must acquire their scale through bootstrapping and function generalization. A slowly changing target can delay successive corrections, especially with gamma one and no discount attenuation of tail error. This does NOT imply a hard propagation depth, a 75-times-EMA training requirement, or an exact network learning time: shared nonlinear parameters can change many time slices together, and the online weights are not fixed during training. Target parameters mixing by 84.7% does not mean 84.7% of a return has been learned.

Consequently, failure of a fresh-Q arm to reach <=5% error in every held-out band after 750 updates would be heavily confounded by initialization, target lag, optimization, and state coverage. It could assess initialization under a fixed compute budget, but it would not isolate the cause of failure or justify blocking policy learning. My earlier absolute gate was too strong for the evidence. Increasing the arbitrary TD budget would not resolve that experimental ambiguity.

Use one simpler staged experiment instead:

1. Fit one shared state-only Phi directly to ALL five complete carry trajectories: G_t^carry = sum of their own remaining interval rewards, with gamma one and no warmup contribution. These are sampled V^carry(s_t) = Q^carry(s_t,P(a_t)) labels under the recorded carry continuation, not Q*, labels for other requests, or local-policy returns. Include time/remaining horizon and keep equal 20% scenario sampling. This direct supervised target supplies the entire return scale without waiting for terminal information to bootstrap backward. Report fitted errors by scenario/horizon; these trajectories are now TRAINING data and cannot be described as independent holdouts.
2. Freeze Phi; initialize both residual output heads to zero and copy online parameters exactly into both targets AFTER initialization. Then Q_i = Phi + A_i starts at the fitted carry scale. On the new local transitions, perform a capped critic-only phase using the existing projected-request encoding and the one-step target y_A = r - Phi(s) + (1-d)[Phi(s') + min_j A_target,j(s',P(a'))], with pi_target = carry, no smoothing, and explicit terminal masking. This learns a correction from real local requests followed hypothetically by carry. The next-state carry value is still estimated, not an observed counterfactual. A carry anchor loss against G^carry-Phi can retain the fitted baseline during this carry-target phase; it must not later be relabeled as a new-policy MC target.
3. Make ONE shared actor proposal from those critics, starting from zero incremental carry and using the stated projected-request transform and bounded output. Freeze the proposal as pi1 before any new collection. A small step budget bounds intervention and computation; it does not certify the actor gradient. Do not require an arbitrary tight all-horizon offline error threshold before this deliberately provisional data-collection step. Retain finite-value, request-bound, identity, and physical execution checks.
4. Run one new-profile TRAINING episode per scenario under the SAME frozen pi1: five complete 75-step episodes, 375 actual transitions total, no within-wave updates, no canonical scoring. Preserve all outcomes and predeclared trajectory-health/stop criteria. A terminal-inventory ratio to carry requires a carry on that SAME profile; absent that comparator, use prospective absolute health limits and report raw TTT/inventory without an improvement claim. Existing carries on different profiles are not matched controls.
5. Only AFTER that wave's readout, use its realized G^pi1 as on-policy sample targets for a capped residual update A_i(s,b_pi1) toward G^pi1-Phi(s), still with equal 20% scenario sampling. This is actual learning from the proposed policy's own continuation. Freeze/review that update before any further actor proposal; another policy change requires another explicitly bounded data decision. Do not automatically extend collection or refit Phi. This establishes a short policy-iteration path rather than repeated offline calibration barriers.

Illustrative bounds for the parent to choose or replace BEFORE execution: up to 1000 carry-supervised updates, 250 local residual critic updates, ten actor steps, the single five-episode wave, and up to 250 post-wave on-policy supervised critic updates. Use one predeclared seed and one candidate, not a seed/arm tournament. Keep gamma one, true terminals, terminal exposure, unchanged physical fallback, and all original data/source preservation rules. These caps are resource limits, not asserted convergence times. Diagnose numerical divergence or failure even to learn the carry return scale; report residual bias and action saturation without treating a hand-picked percentage as a policy theorem.

Validation must follow the continuation actually estimated. Before the wave, the residual critic targets Q^carry after its initial request, so comparing it with G^pi1 does NOT independently measure Q^pi1 calibration. The wave directly validates realized pi1 behavior and supplies honest on-policy labels; fitting those labels afterward yields training error, not independent validation of the newly fitted critic. Five fresh profiles improve evidence about the proposal but provide no strong generalization or optimality guarantee. A healthy, valid wave can justify the bounded on-policy update without first demanding that an old-policy critic predict a changed continuation accurately.

The counterpoint remains decisive: request-level encoding removes nominal aliases, but carry supervision constrains Q only on the carry-action slice, and two trajectories do not identify neighboring action derivatives. A state-only Phi supplies no action-ranking evidence. Small own-target residuals, agreeing twins, and a numerically well-scaled baseline do not fix that. Limit the actor to one explicit proposal, inspect distinct projected requests and saturation, and let new sequential outcomes inform the next update. The scientific claim at this stage is successful return-scale initialization followed by measured behavior and on-policy learning, not demonstrated traffic improvement.
